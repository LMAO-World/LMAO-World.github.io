Abstract

Reinforcement learning can improve vision-language-action policies beyond imitation, but humanoid exploration is unsafe and simulator RL can exploit residual sim-to-real gaps. We present LMAO-World, a teleoperation-free framework for post-training a whole-body humanoid VLA in an action-conditioned video world model. Simulated manipulation and locomotion play data improve contact-dynamics and viewpoint-change prediction. A VLA trained on synthetic demonstrations collects 20 autonomous real robot rollouts per task to align the video WM with deployment observations. The VLA, whole-body controller, and video WM then form a closed-loop RL environment with dense progress rewards. Across three real robot tasks, video WM RL raises mean success from 23.3% to 75.0%, compared with 48.3% for real robot RL. The system leverages complementary environments: simulation provides policy initialization and broad coverage, while the video WM provides a deployment-aligned environment for policy improvement.

01

Sim to dream to real

Complementary roles for simulation and video WM

1

Initialize in simulation

The LEGS simulator generates 200 whole-body demonstrations per task for base-VLA training and physically grounded play data for broad dynamics coverage.

2

Align the video WM

Each task uses 20 autonomous real robot rollouts, balanced between successes and failures, to align generated observations with deployment.

3

Improve the policy

The current VLA generates 480 on-policy video WM rollouts, while stage-aware progress supplies dense rewards without further robot interaction.

02

LEGS simulator

Task demonstrations and play data

Full-state planning supplies task data, while autonomous play data covers contact outcomes and locomotion-induced viewpoint changes.

TASK DATA

Synthetic demonstrations

Whole-body action sequences are generated from full-state planning and locomotion and manipulation primitives.

PLAY DATA

Manipulation

Randomized objects, grasp offsets, slips, misses, and collisions provide broad supervision for contact dynamics.

PLAY DATA

Locomotion

Approaches, turns, and long-range walking expose the model to large egocentric viewpoint changes.

Deployment-aligned video WM

Replaying real actions through generated observations

We replay held-out action trajectories and compare autoregressive video WM predictions with temporally aligned ground-truth observations.

06

How the two environments work together

Initialization

Synthetic demonstrations

Photorealistic simulation trains a whole-body base policy with enough hardware success to collect autonomous deployment experience.

Coverage

Play data

Physically grounded manipulation and locomotion experience improves contact dynamics and consistency under large viewpoint changes.

Alignment

Real robot rollouts

Limited autonomous successes and failures align the video WM with deployment observations and cross the remaining visual gap.

Improvement

Dense on-policy RL

Dense progress and on-policy training extract useful signal from successful, failed, and suboptimal video WM rollouts.
